Origins & Evolution

Tortoises did not merely survive deep time — they are deep time, wearing it as armor.

The shell did not appear all at once. It began as a modification of the ribcage: broadened, flattened ribs that gradually fused outward into a rigid case, trading rib-cage flexibility (and the fast breathing it allows) for a portable fortress. That trade has now paid off for a quarter of a billion years.

Present day

~50+ living Testudinidae species

True land tortoises persist on every continent except Antarctica and Australia, from Saharan scrub to Mediterranean hillsides to Pacific island atolls.

~50–60 million years ago · Eocene

Testudinidae emerges

The family of true land tortoises radiates outward from Asia, later reaching Africa, the Americas, and — by rafting on ocean currents — remote islands such as the Galápagos and Aldabra, where isolation produced giants.

~66 million years ago

The K–Pg extinction

The asteroid impact that ended the age of dinosaurs wiped out roughly three-quarters of Earth's species. Turtles and tortoises — already shelled, already slow-metabolism survivors — were among the lineages that made it through.

~220 million years ago · Triassic

Proganochelys quenstedti

One of the earliest known fully-shelled turtles. It already carried a complete carapace and plastron, but unlike modern tortoises it could not withdraw its head or limbs — that defense was still to be evolved.

~260 million years ago · Permian

The proto-shell

Ancestral reptiles with unusually wide, T-shaped ribs mark the earliest fossil evidence of the anatomical shift that would, over tens of millions of years, become the shell.

Why the name "tortoise"

English draws the word from Old French tortue/tortre, itself likely from Late Latin tortuca — possibly linked to tortus, "twisted," a nod to the animal's bowed legs. The Galápagos Islands themselves take their name from the tortoises: galápago is an old Spanish word for a type of saddle, which early sailors thought the domed shells resembled.

The Elders — Maximum Lifespan & the Oldest Ever Recorded

No land animal outlives a tortoise. The record for oldest-ever is not close.

Jonathan

Seychelles giant tortoise (Aldabrachelys gigantea hololissa). Believed born around 1832, gifted to St Helena's governor in 1882 already fully mature. He turned 193 on 4 December 2025 and is recognized as the oldest living land animal ever documented — and the oldest chelonian (the group spanning tortoises, turtles, and terrapins) in recorded history.

Tu'i Malila

A radiated tortoise presented to the royal family of Tonga by Captain Cook around 1777. She remained in the royal household until her death in 1965 at an estimated 188 years old — the record Jonathan eventually surpassed.

"On mild days, he will sunbathe — his long neck and legs stretched fully out of his shell to absorb heat and transfer it to his core." In cold weather he instead digs into leaf mould and stays there all day.— Joe Hollins, Jonathan's veterinarian, via Guinness World Records

Can size or weight tell you a tortoise's age?

Only loosely, and only while it's still growing. The traditional method — counting the growth rings on the shell (scutes) — is now well documented as unreliable past early juvenile age: peer-reviewed studies on several species found the "one ring per year" assumption holds up reasonably well only to about 5–6 years old. Beyond that, tortoises can lay down several rings in a good season, none in a lean one, and wear old rings smooth — so the count stops tracking calendar years. Weight and shell length are a fair proxy for growth stage while an animal is young, but once a tortoise reads as full adult size, it could plausibly be anywhere from its species' age of maturity to 100+ years old — size simply stops being informative.

Peso òf tamaño por bisa e edat di un kawama di tera?

Solamente mas o menos, i solamente miéntras e ta sigui krisiendo. E método tradishonal — konta e ringnan di krisimentu riba e kaska (scutes) — awor ta bon dokumentá komo nò konfiabel despues di edat huvenil tempran: studionan revisá pa perito riba vários species a haña ku e suposishon "un ring pa aña" ta funshoná mas o menos bon solamente te mas o menos 5–6 aña bieu. Despues di esei, kawama por pone vários ring den un temporada bon, ningun den un mal, i e ringnan bieu por wòrdu gastá liso — pues e konteo ta stòp di sigui aña kalendario. Peso i largura di kaska ta un bon indikashon di etapa di krisimentu miéntras e animal ta hóben, pero ora un kawama parse tamaño kompleto di adulto, e por ta kaminda ku sea for di edat di madurez di su species te 100+ aña bieu — tamaño simplemente stòp di duna informashon.

Why Tortoises Matter

A slow animal can still be load-bearing for an entire ecosystem.

Ecosystem engineers

Burrowing species like the North American desert tortoise (Gopherus agassizii) and the closely related gopher tortoise dig deep, temperature-stable burrows that they do not keep to themselves. Hundreds of other species — burrowing owls, indigo snakes, rabbits, insects, and more — shelter in abandoned or shared tortoise burrows, especially during fire, drought, or extreme heat. Remove the tortoise and an entire underground housing system for the local food web disappears with it.

Seed dispersers

Giant tortoises on the Galápagos and Aldabra act as slow-motion gardeners: they range widely, eat large quantities of fruit and vegetation, and pass viable seeds — some from large-seeded plants that few other living animals can disperse — over long distances in their droppings. In places where larger native seed-dispersing animals have gone extinct, giant tortoises are one of the last species still doing that job.

Indicator species

Because tortoises are long-lived, site-faithful, and sensitive to habitat quality, temperature, and food availability, population health in a given tortoise species is a useful proxy for the health of the broader habitat around it.

Cultural and symbolic weight

Few animals carry as much symbolic freight. Aesop's fable made the tortoise a byword for patience over speed. In Hindu cosmology, Kurma — a tortoise — is one of the avatars of Vishnu, supporting the world on its back during the churning of the cosmic ocean. Related "world turtle" motifs appear across multiple traditions. The Galápagos Islands are literally named after their tortoises. A slow-moving reptile has, repeatedly and independently, become a stand-in for endurance itself.

Conservation flagships — and casualties

That same visibility makes tortoises powerful conservation ambassadors, but many species are in genuine danger. The radiated tortoise of Madagascar — the species Tu'i Malila belonged to — is Critically Endangered, driven down by habitat loss and an illegal pet trade that has emptied wild populations by the tens of thousands. Several Galápagos giant tortoise subspecies were pushed to the edge of extinction by 19th-century sailors, who stacked live tortoises in ship holds as a self-sustaining, no-feed, no-water meat supply — a grim footnote in the same history that produced Jonathan's own story.

Egg to Hatchling

Everything about a tortoise's long life starts in a shallow hole in warm ground.

Step 1 — Nesting

The female digs a nest chamber

Using her hind legs alone, she excavates a flask-shaped chamber in sun-warmed soil, then lays a clutch ranging from a handful of eggs in small species to 15–30+ in large ones, depending on species and body size.

Step 2 — Incubation

Temperature decides more than time

Incubation runs roughly 60–120+ days depending on species and temperature. In most tortoises, incubation temperature also determines the hatchlings' sex — temperature-dependent sex determination — with warmer nests generally producing more females and cooler nests more males.

Step 3 — Pipping

The egg tooth does its one job

A hatchling uses a temporary, sharp caruncle ("egg tooth") on its snout to slice through the leathery shell. Pipping to full emergence can take hours to a couple of days — patience, not force, gets it out.

Step 4 — Yolk absorption

The first meal is already inside it

Hatchlings still carry an internal yolk sac supplying nutrition for their first several days. They typically should not be fed until that reserve is absorbed — usually 2–5 days after hatching.

Step 5 — First husbandry

Warmth, humidity, and calcium from day one

Hatchlings need a warm basking spot, appropriate humidity for the species (too dry or too wet both cause problems), full-spectrum UVB lighting or safe natural sun exposure, and a shallow water dish for regular soaking — they dehydrate far faster than adults.

Ongoing — Growth-phase feeding

Daily meals, careful calcium, controlled growth

Young, growing tortoises are typically fed daily with extra calcium support. Growth should be tracked (weight and shell length) against expected curves — too-fast growth from overfeeding or excess protein is a leading cause of pyramiding (raised, bumpy scutes) and other shell deformities.

Usando solamente su pianan patras, e ta koba un kamber na forma di botter den suela kayentá pa solo, despues e ta pone un cluster ku por ta di un poko webu den species chikitu te 15–30+ den esnan grandi, dependiendo di species i tamaño di kurpa.

Temperatura ta disidí mas ku tempu

Inkubashon ta dura mas o menos 60–120+ dia dependiendo di species i temperatura. Den mayoria di kawama di tera, temperatura di inkubashon tambe ta disidí sekso di e kriaturanan — determinashon di sekso dependiente di temperatura — ku landuranan mas kayente generalmente ta produsí mas hembra i esnan mas fresku mas macho.

Paso 3 — Kibra kaska

E djente di webu ta hasi su úniko trabou

Un kriatura ta usa un caruncle skèrpi temporal ("djente di webu") riba su nanishi pa kòrta e kaska duru. For di kibra kaska te kompletamente sali por dura for di ora te un par di dia — pasenshi, no fòrsa, ta saka e kriatura fó.

Paso 4 — Absorshon di yema

E promé kuminda ta ya paden

Kriaturanan ainda ta karga un saku di yema paden ku ta duna nutrishon pa nan promé dianan. Normalmente nan no mester wòrdu alimentá te ora e reserva ei wòrdu absorbí — normalmente 2–5 dia despues di sali for di webu.

Paso 5 — Promé kuido

Kalor, humedat, i kalsio for di dia un

Kriaturanan mester un lugá kayente pa tuma solo, humedat apropiado pa e species (muchu seku òf muchu muhá tur dos ta kousa problema), lus UVB spektro kompleto òf eksposishon di solo natural sigur, i un bandeha di awa dòf pa plèk regular — nan ta seka hopi mas lihé ku adulto.

Kontinuo — Alimentashon den fase di krisimentu

Kuminda tur dia, kalsio ku kuidou, krisimentu kontrolá

Kawama hóben ku ta krisiendo normalmente ta wòrdu alimentá tur dia ku sosten extra di kalsio. Substrate

Organic topsoil (no fertilizers or pesticides), coco coir, or a topsoil/sand blend suits most species; loose sand alone is best avoided, especially for hatchlings, since ingested particles during feeding can cause intestinal impaction. Substrate should hold enough moisture to support natural burrowing and humidity without staying waterlogged.

Space, growth, and enclosure sizing

Enclosures need to scale with the animal — a sulcata or Aldabra tortoise that starts the size of a coin will eventually need yards of outdoor space, not a tank. Undersized, permanent indoor housing is one of the most common welfare failures in tortoise keeping.

Brumation

Temperate species — Russian, Hermann's, Greek — evolved with a true winter and will brumate (a reptile's version of hibernation) if conditions call for it; done incorrectly, brumation is dangerous, so temperature and health need veterinary-level attention beforehand. Desert and tropical species generally do not require brumation and should stay warm year-round.

Substrato

Tera órgániko (sin fertilisante ni pestisida), fibra di koko, òf un mesun tera/santu ta bon pa mayoria di species; santu suelto solo ta mihó evitá, spesialmente pa kriaturanan, pasobra partikulanan ingerí durante kuminda por kousa impakshon intestinal. Substrato mester tene sufisiente humedat pa sosten koba natural i humedat sin keda muhá permanentemente.

Espasio, krisimentu, i tamaño di sirkumbenshon

Sirkumbenshonnan mester krese huntu ku e animal — un kawama sulcata òf Aldabra ku ta kuminsá tamaño di un plaka lo eventualmente mester yardanan di espasio pafó, no un tanki. Kas paden, permanente i muchu chikitu, ta un di e fayonan di bienestar mas komun den kuido di kawama di tera.

Brumashon

Species di klima temperado — Ruso, Hermann's, Griego — a evolushoná ku un invierno real i lo brumá (e vershon di reptil di hibernashon) si kondishonnan ta pidié; hasí robes, brumashon ta peligroso, pues temperatura i salú mester atenshon di nivel veterinario di antemano. Species di desierto i tropikal generalmente no mester brumashon i mester keda kayente henter aña.

Sun Exposure

Sunlight is not ambience for a tortoise — it is the trigger for building its own skeleton and shell.

Why UVB is non-negotiable

Ultraviolet-B light lets a tortoise's skin synthesize vitamin D3, which in turn is required to absorb dietary calcium. Without adequate UVB, calcium intake barely matters — the body cannot use it, leading to metabolic bone disease and soft, deformed shells. Ordinary window glass and most plastics filter out UVB almost entirely, so a tortoise basking indoors "in the sun" through a window is not getting the wavelength it actually needs.

Natural sun vs. bulbs

Unfiltered outdoor sunlight is the gold standard and free. Indoor-only tortoises need a dedicated reptile UVB bulb (commonly a T5 high-output tube) positioned at the manufacturer's recommended distance, replaced on schedule — UVB output fades well before the bulb stops producing visible light, so a bulb can look fine and still be nutritionally useless.

Basking behavior

Tortoises thermoregulate by moving, not by internal control — they bask to raise body temperature and retreat to shade or burrows to cool down. Jonathan's veterinarian describes exactly this: on mild days, stretching neck and legs fully out of the shell to soak up heat; on cold days, burying into leaf litter instead. Any enclosure, indoor or outdoor, needs both a genuine basking zone and a genuine cool/shaded retreat so the animal can regulate itself.

Solo no ta ambiente numa pa un kawama di tera — e ta e señal pa konstruí su propio eskeleto i kaska.

Dikon UVB no ta negosiabel

Lus ultravioleta-B ta laga e kueru di kawama sintetisá vitamina D3, ku na su turno ta nesesario pa absorbe kalsio for di dieta. Sin sufisiente UVB, konsumo di kalsio kasi no ta importá — e kurpa no por usé, ta hiba na malesa metabóliko di wesu i kaska blandu i deformá. Glas normal di bentana i mayoria plástiko ta filtra UVB kasi kompletamente, pues un kawama ku ta tuma "solo" paden dor di un bentana no ta risibí e onda ku e realmente mester.

Solo natural vs. lampi

Solo pafó sin filtro ta e estándar di oro i ta gratis. Kawama ku ta biba solamente paden mester un lampi UVB dediká pa reptil (komunmente un tubo T5 di haltu produkshon) posishoná na e distansia rekomendá pa fabrikante, kambiá segun horario — produkshon di UVB ta baha muchu promé ku e lampi stòp di produsí lus visibel, pues un lampi por parse bon i tòg no tin balor nutrishonal.

Komportashon di tuma solo

Kawama di tera ta regulá nan temperatura dor di move, no dor di kontrol interno — nan ta tuma solo pa hisa temperatura di kurpa i bai den sombra òf buraku pa friu. Doktor bèt di Jonathan ta deskribí eksaktamente esaki: riba dianan suave, ekstendé garganta i pianan kompletamente for di e kaska pa absorbe kalor; riba dianan friu, kaba den blachi seku en bes di. Kualke sirkumbenshon, paden òf pafó, mester tantu un zona di tuma solo real komo un lugá fresku/ku sombra real pa e animal por regulá su mes.

Water & Swimming

Tortoises and turtles get lumped together constantly — in water, that mix-up can kill a tortoise.

Can tortoises swim?

Not well, and not like aquatic turtles. Turtles have flattened, streamlined shells and paddle-like limbs built for buoyancy and propulsion. Tortoises have heavy, high-domed shells and stumpy, column-like legs built for walking, not floating. Dropped into water deeper than they can stand in, a tortoise can struggle, exhaust itself, and drown — it is not a natural swimmer finding its footing, it is a land animal in the wrong medium.

Drinking and hydration

In the wild, tortoises often drink opportunistically from puddles after rain, sometimes even appearing to anticipate rainfall. In captivity, they should always have access to a shallow, sturdy water dish they can climb in and out of easily, filled no deeper than the underside of their shell.

Soaking — the actual water need

Regular shallow soaks (lukewarm water, head kept comfortably above the surface) support hydration, help with shedding and defecation, and are especially important for hatchlings and young tortoises, who dehydrate far faster than adults and benefit from soaks several times a week. Any water feature in an enclosure needs an easy, sloped exit — never a sheer-sided container a tortoise could tip into and be unable to climb out of.

Kawama di tera i kawama di laman ta wòrdu konfundí konstantemente — den awa, e konfushon ei por mata un kawama di tera.

Kawama di tera por landa?

No bon, i no manera kawama akwátiko. Kawama di laman tin kaska plat, aerodinámiko i pianan manera remo trahá pa flotashon i propulshon. Kawama di tera tin kaska pisá, haltu i redondo i pianan stòmpi manera koló trahá pa kana, no pa floto. Kaí den awa mas profundo ku nan por para den dje, un kawama di tera por lucha, kansa su mes kompletamente, i hoga — e no ta un nadadó natural buskando su pia, e ta un bestia di tera den e medio robes.

Bebementu i hidratashon

Den naturalesa, kawama di tera hopi biaha ta bebe oportunistikamente for di poso di awa despues di áwaseru, algun biaha te aparentemente antisipando áwaseru. Den kautiverio, nan mester semper tin akseso na un bandeha di awa dòf i firmi ku nan por subi paden i sali fasilmente, yená no mas profundo ku e parti abou di nan kaska.

Plèk den awa — e nesesidat di awa real

Plèk regular i dòf (awa tibi, kabes keda kómodamente ariba di e superfisie) ta yuda ku hidratashon, ta yuda ku muda di kueru i defekashon, i ta importante spesialmente pa kriaturanan i kawama hóben, ku ta seka hopi mas lihé ku adulto i ta benefisiá for di plèk vários biaha pa siman. Kualke elemento di awa den un sirkumbenshon mester tin un salida fásil i ku pendiente — nunka un kontenedor ku banda dreit kaminda un kawama por kai paden i no por sali fó atrobe.

Diet, Feeding & Nutrition

Most captive tortoise health problems trace back to one thing: a diet that looks like a salad bar instead of a scrubby field.

The wild baseline

Wild tortoises graze low-calorie, high-fiber, calcium-rich weeds and grasses growing in mineral-poor, calcium-heavy soils — plants most people would consider poor eating. That profile, not supermarket produce, is the target for a healthy captive diet.

Mayoria di problema di salú di kawama di tera den kautiverio ta bini for di un kos: un dieta ku ta parse un bar di salad en bes di un mondi seku.

E base salbahe

Kawama di tera salbahe ta kome yerba i mata di baho kalorí, halto fibra, rijo na kalsio ku ta krese den suela pober na mineral pero rijo na kalsio — mata ku mayoria hende lo konsiderá mal kuminda. E perfil ei, no produkto di supermerkado, ta e meta pa un dieta sano den kautiverio.

Field note — Asystasia gangeticaNota di kampo — Asystasia gangetica

Also known as Chinese violet or creeping foxglove, this low sprawling groundcover (purple and white five-petal flowers, above) is a good example of appropriate forage: palatable, high in fiber, and calcium-rich relative to phosphorus. Widely fed to sulcata, leopard, and other grazing species as part of a varied diet — never as a sole food source — and only from pesticide-free growing areas.Tambe konosí komo "Chinese violet", e mata di baho ku ta plama abou (flor mòrá i blanku ku sinku pétalo, ariba) ta un bon ehèmpel di kuminda apropiado: sabroso, halto na fibra, i rijo na kalsio relativo na fósforo. Duná hopi na sulcata, leopardo, i otro species ku ta kome yerba komo parti di un dieta variá — nunka komo úniko fuente di kuminda — i solamente for di lugá ku no ta usa pestisida.

Portion size

A widely used veterinary rule of thumb: feed roughly 1–4% of body weight per day. Smaller and juvenile tortoises sit toward the higher end of that range because of faster growth and metabolism; large adult tortoises need a smaller percentage of their (much greater) body weight.

Daily, or not?

It depends on age and species. Young, actively growing tortoises generally do best fed daily, with attentive calcium supplementation. Many adult tortoises, especially smaller Mediterranean species, do well on 3–4 feedings a week rather than daily meals. Tortoises with year-round outdoor grazing access are effectively "fed daily" through pasture, with supplemental feeding used mainly to fill gaps.

Foods to avoid or limit

High-oxalate greens (spinach, beet greens, chard) bind calcium into a form the body cannot use if fed too often. Brassica-family vegetables (cabbage, kale, broccoli) in excess can interfere with iodine uptake. Processed, high-sugar, or high-protein "treat" foods — including most fruit beyond an occasional small portion — should stay occasional, not staple.

Tamaño di porshon

Un regla di dedo veterinario hopi usá: duna mas o menos 1–4% di peso di kurpa pa dia. Kawama chikitu i huvenil ta sinta mas serka di e parti halto di e rango ei pa motibu di krisimentu i metabolismo mas lihé; kawama adulto grandi mester un porshentahe mas chikitu di nan peso (muchu mas grandi) di kurpa.

Tur dia, òf no?

E ta dependé di edat i species. Kawama hóben ku ta krisiendo aktivamente generalmente ta hasi mihó ku kuminda tur dia, ku suplementashon di kalsio ku atenshon. Hopi kawama adulto, spesialmente species Mediteráneo mas chikitu, ta hasi bon ku 3–4 kuminda pa siman en bes di kuminda tur dia. Kawama ku tin akseso pafó pa kome henter aña ta efektivamente "wòrdu alimentá tur dia" via pastu, ku alimentashon suplementario usá primeramente pa yena vaсío.

Kuminda pa evitá òf limitá

Bèrdenan ku halto oxalato (spinasi, bèrde di remolacha, chard) ta mará kalsio den un forma ku e kurpa no por usa si duná muchu tantu biaha. Bèrdenan di famia Brassica (kool, kale, broccoli) den eksesivo por interferí ku absorshon di yodo. Kuminda prosesá, halto na suku, òf halto na proteina komo "delikatèsa" — inkluyendo mayoria fruta pasa un porshon chikitu okashonal — mester keda okashonal, no básiko.
